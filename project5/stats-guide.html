What Do All These Stats Mean?

If you’ve ever looked at a stat sheet and had no idea what half the numbers meant, this page is for you. Here’s a breakdown of what each stat actually tells you.

The Basics

Points
How many points a player scores.
Rebounds
How many times a player grabs the ball after a missed shot, on either end of the court.
Assists
How many times a player passes the ball to a teammate who then scores.
Steals
How many times a player takes the ball away from the other team.
Blocks
How many times a player stops an opponent’s shot from going in.
Turnovers
When a player loses possession of the ball to the other team because of a mistake, such as a bad pass or losing control of the ball.

These are the numbers you’ll see on a normal stat sheet, and they’re a good starting point for judging how a player performed in a game.

The Advanced Stuff

Basic stats only tell part of the story, so analysts came up with a few more advanced numbers to fill in the gaps.

Player Efficiency Rating (PER)

Player Efficiency Rating (PER) tries to boil down everything a player does into one number. It was created by sportswriter John Hollinger, and it adds up a player’s good plays, like scoring, rebounds, and assists, and subtracts the bad ones, like missed shots and turnovers, then adjusts the total based on how many minutes they played (Basketball-Reference, “Calculating PER,” basketball-reference.com/about/per.html). The league average PER is always set at 15.00, so anything above that means a player performed better than an average NBA player that season (Wikipedia, “Player efficiency rating”).

True Shooting Percentage (TS%)

True shooting percentage (TS%) measures how efficient a player is at scoring. It’s more accurate than a normal shooting percentage because it factors in free throws and three pointers, not just regular field goals (Basketball-Reference, “Glossary,” basketball-reference.com/about/glossary.html). Some of the most efficient scorers in NBA history, like Stephen Curry and Kevin Durant, have career true shooting percentages above 62 percent (Wikipedia, “True shooting percentage”).

Usage Rate

Usage rate estimates how much of a team’s offense runs through a player while they’re on the court, based on how often they shoot, get to the free throw line, or turn the ball over compared to the rest of the team (Basketball-Reference, “Glossary,” basketball-reference.com/about/glossary.html). If a player has a high usage rate and a high true shooting percentage, that usually means they’re a real star, since they can handle a lot of the offense without losing efficiency.
